\begin{abstract}
Spiking Transformers avoid dense floating-point attention, but it remains
unclear whether binary query--key spike patterns form a useful discrete
lookup-table (LUT) address space. We study this question in QKFormer through a
staged diagnostic and frozen-backbone adapter pipeline rather than claiming a
full hardware LUT replacement. On CIFAR-10, Q/K-addressed prototypes reduce
held-out response MSE by $3.58\%$ relative to a global mean. Across independently
trained single-step ($T=1$) checkpoints, early Q-K attention modules retain
near-complete address coverage, although residual-adapter accuracy changes are
checkpoint- and stage-dependent. We further train a $T=1$ CIFAR-100 baseline to
$77.76\%$ top-1 accuracy. On CIFAR-100, the address LUT improves top-1 accuracy
by $0.163$ points on average and outperforms token/channel and same-capacity
shuffled-address controls, but global-mean smoothing is stronger at $+0.323$
points. Decomposing the adapter into a global component and a zero-mean address
residual yields $+0.197$ points versus $-0.170$ for its matched shuffled
control, further isolating address alignment without surpassing the global
baseline. These results establish that Q/K address alignment contains
measurable response structure, while also showing that generic smoothing is a
strong classification baseline. The supported claim is therefore structured,
low-disturbance LUT adaptation rather than checkpoint-stable accuracy gains,
energy or latency improvements, or a production full-LUT wrapper.
\end{abstract}
