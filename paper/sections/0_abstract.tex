\begin{abstract}
Spiking Transformers replace dense floating-point attention with event-driven
operations, but it remains unclear whether their binary query--key states form
reliable lookup-table (LUT) addresses. We study this question in QKFormer with
a staged, negative-controlled audit that separates address feasibility,
held-out response reconstruction, support-aware fallback, and downstream
adapter utility. Q/K-addressed prototypes reduce held-out response MSE relative
to a global mean by $3.71\%\pm0.004$ on CIFAR-10 and $4.42\%\pm0.004$ on
CIFAR-100 across randomized calibration subsets. Coarse token/channel LUTs
reach only $2.79\%$ and $3.42\%$, while shuffling the address--prototype
alignment degrades MSE by $17.83\%$ and $15.82\%$, respectively. Across three
independently trained single-step CIFAR-10 checkpoints, correct-address
reductions range from $3.76\%$ to $5.94\%$ and exceed token/channel controls in
every case. To address the dimensionality objection against monolithic Q/K
tables, we introduce a hierarchical backoff LUT that searches full-address,
partial-address, token/channel, and global prototypes with explicit support
thresholds. On CIFAR-100, the hierarchy beats token/channel lookup for every
calibration seed from 8 to 512 batches and retains the full-address
reconstruction gain, but its active compact-table footprint remains
$40.4\%$--$51.1\%$, so we do not claim solved LUT compression. Frozen residual
adapter probes show that address alignment can beat token/channel and shuffled
controls, but global smoothing remains a strong classification baseline and
accuracy shifts are checkpoint- and stage-dependent. Thus QK-LUTFormer
establishes reproducible, interpretable Q/K lookup-addressability, while
explicitly ruling out unsupported claims about stable accuracy gains, energy,
latency, or production full-LUT replacement.
\end{abstract}
