\begin{abstract}
Spiking Transformers expose binary internal states, but it remains unclear
whether those states form reliable lookup-table (LUT) addresses or merely
convenient binary codes. We introduce QK-LUTFormer, a negative-controlled
framework for studying lookup addressability in spiking attention. It combines
code-faithful Q/K addresses, global-residual prototypes, explicit
support-aware backoff, compact semantic subtables, and a
Temporal-Channel Calibrated Semantic Lookup Unit (TCSLU) that restores the
current statistics presented to downstream BN/LIF dynamics. Correct Q/K
alignment reduces held-out response MSE by $3.71\%$ on CIFAR-10 and $4.42\%$
on CIFAR-100, compared with $2.79\%$ and $3.42\%$ for coarse token/channel
lookup, while shuffled alignment degrades MSE by $17.83\%$ and $15.82\%$.
A subspace-decoupled TC+QK estimator retains $94.31\%$ of the full-address
reconstruction gain using $20.54\%$ of the compact address-space entry count;
its matched shuffled control is worse than the global mean. These results show
that the predictive structure is address-aligned and compositionally compact.
Local reconstruction alone is insufficient for spiking inference, however:
static lookup can shift current statistics that are amplified by thresholded
dynamics. TCSLU closes this gap, preserving QKFormer accuracy within $0.44$
percentage points on CIFAR-10/100 at $T=1$ and $T=4$. Across five fixed
calibration subsets, aligned lookup ranks first and has paired 95\% intervals
excluding zero against global and token/channel controls. Finally, an
interaction-level intervention shows that lookup compatibility is conditioned
on a discrete QK contract: adapting Spikformer to a
$\operatorname{gate}(Q)\odot K$ interaction reduces the calibrated replacement
drop to $1.38$ points, whereas the unmodified architecture remains a mismatch
boundary. On CIFAR10-DVS, a LUT-only QKFormer deployment reaches $83.9\%$,
while a separately trained SpiLiFormer-2-256 LUT transfer reaches $81.7\%$
from its paired $81.2\%$ teacher. The latter demonstrates executable
cross-architecture substitution but remains below the registered $84.0\%$
gate because the from-scratch teacher did not reproduce the published
$86.7\%$. The evidence supports a semantic-addressability and dynamic-contract
principle for QK-compatible spiking attention, not a general law of spiking
neurons or a claim of measured SRAM, latency, energy, ImageNet, or production
hardware gains.
\end{abstract}
