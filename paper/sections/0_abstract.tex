\begin{abstract}
Spiking Transformers avoid dense floating-point attention, but it remains
unclear whether their binary query--key spike patterns can serve as a useful
discrete lookup-table (LUT) address space. We study this question in QKFormer,
a hierarchical spiking Transformer whose Q-K attention already exposes binary
query/key spike vectors and token/channel gates. Instead of claiming a full
hardware LUT replacement, we evaluate whether Q/K-derived addresses explain and
improve local attention-module responses under controlled diagnostics. On
CIFAR-10, trained QKFormer checkpoints produce well occupied Q/K address
buckets. A split-aware reconstruction diagnostic reduces response MSE by
$3.58\%$ relative to a global mean baseline, with the strongest gain in stage 1.
Forward-only prototype replacement is only weakly address-specific, motivating
a frozen-backbone trainable residual LUT adapter. In a single-step ($T=1$)
stress setting on an independently trained checkpoint, an alpha sensitivity
sweep identifies a low-disturbance operating point: at $\alpha=0.025$, the
Q/K-addressed adapter improves top-1 accuracy across three adapter seeds
($+0.14$ mean delta), outperforming global smoothing ($+0.04$) and
token/channel-only LUTs ($0.00$). Larger residual weights drift and become
negative. These results support Q/K binary addresses as a meaningful structure
for small residual LUT adapters, while leaving energy, latency, ImageNet, and
full-wrapper claims to future experiments.
\end{abstract}
