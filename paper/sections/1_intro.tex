\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Spiking neural networks (SNNs) are attractive for event-driven and low-power
inference, but modern visual recognition increasingly depends on Transformer
attention. Directly translating dense softmax attention into spikes is
difficult: attention weights depend on continuous query--key scores and on
row-wise normalization over all tokens. Recent spiking Transformers therefore
often redesign attention rather than convert standard attention literally.

QKFormer~\cite{zhou2024qkformer} is a strong example of this design direction.
Its spike-form Q-K attention uses binary vectors to model token or channel
importance and obtains a hierarchical spiking Transformer. This design raises a
different question from standard ANN-to-SNN conversion: if Q/K spike vectors
are already binary, can they form a useful discrete address space for lookup
modules?

This paper studies that question through QK-LUTFormer. We do not claim a pure
LUT hardware replacement for the full QKFormer block. Instead, we build a
diagnostic and adapter pipeline that asks whether Q/K binary addresses explain
attention-module responses better than non-address controls. To address the
obvious dimensionality concern of direct full-vector lookup, we additionally
evaluate a support-aware hierarchical backoff LUT that searches from fine
Q/K-address prototypes to coarser token/channel prototypes before falling back
to the global mean. The method freezes a trained QKFormer backbone, calibrates
response prototypes, and trains only tiny residual LUT adapters under
classification, baseline-logit distillation, and local response constraints.

Our experiments show a nuanced picture. Forward-only address prototypes can
slightly improve early-stage replacement, but global smoothing is a strong
control. A $T=1$ stress setting removes temporal averaging and exposes useful
address alignment on one checkpoint, including a negative same-capacity
shuffled control. Additional checkpoints, stages, and CIFAR-100 show that the
classification effect is not universally address-specific: address LUTs often
beat shuffled and token/channel controls, while global smoothing can remain
stronger. This distinction separates evidence for address structure from a
broader accuracy-gain claim.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item We formulate QKFormer Q/K spike vectors as a discrete LUT address
  space and define feasibility diagnostics for coverage, singleton rate, and
  conditional response variance.
  \item We introduce a support-aware hierarchical backoff QK-LUT audit that
  makes lookup misses explicit through full-address, partial-address,
  token/channel, and global fallback levels.
  \item We introduce a frozen-backbone trainable residual LUT adapter that uses
  calibrated Q/K-address prototypes with small trainable correction tables.
  \item We provide a staged ablation study on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, including
  reconstruction diagnostics, forward-only replacement, stage/calibration/blend
  sweeps, independent-checkpoint replication, and T=1 alpha sensitivity
  controls.
  \item We explicitly identify claim boundaries: current results support a
  structured reconstruction and low-disturbance adapter analysis, not
  checkpoint-stable accuracy, compact-table compression, energy, latency,
  ImageNet, or full-wrapper claims.
\end{itemize}
