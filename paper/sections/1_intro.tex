\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Spiking neural networks (SNNs) are attractive for event-driven and low-power
inference, but modern visual recognition increasingly depends on Transformer
attention. Directly translating dense softmax attention into spikes is
difficult: attention weights depend on continuous query--key scores and on
row-wise normalization over all tokens. Recent spiking Transformers therefore
often redesign attention rather than convert standard attention literally.

QKFormer~\cite{zhou2024qkformer} is a strong example of this design direction.
Its spike-form Q-K attention uses binary vectors to model token or channel
importance and obtains a hierarchical spiking Transformer. This design raises a
different question from standard ANN-to-SNN conversion: when do binary
attention states become useful semantic addresses rather than arbitrary codes?

Three obstacles separate binary states from a useful LUT mechanism. First,
binarization alone does not imply that an address predicts a reproducible
response; this requires held-out tests against coarse, random, and
alignment-breaking controls. Second, even predictive addresses can be sparsely
supported or form an impractically large Cartesian table. Third, a locally
accurate prototype can shift current statistics, and small shifts may be
amplified by BatchNorm and thresholded LIF dynamics. A useful account of
lookup-addressability must address all three obstacles rather than equating a
binary representation with a deployable table.

QK-LUTFormer addresses these obstacles as one framework. It first constructs
deterministic mixed-radix addresses from QKFormer's existing head,
token/channel, Q/gate, K, and population states. It then represents each
prediction as a global response plus supported semantic residuals. A
hierarchical rule exposes every fallback, while a subspace-decoupled estimator
replaces product-size full-address storage with token/channel, Q/gate, and K
residual subtables. Finally, TCSLU calibrates the returned current per time step
and channel before the original BN/LIF path. In short, semantic states determine
\emph{what} to retrieve, support-aware residuals determine \emph{when} to trust
it, and temporal-channel calibration determines whether the retrieved current
survives downstream spiking dynamics.

The experiments follow the same causal order. Correct Q/K addresses predict
held-out responses beyond matched controls; explicit backoff makes sparse
support auditable; compact semantic subtables preserve most of the full-address
gain; and TCSLU closes much of the reconstruction-to-classification gap. A
QK-contract Spikformer intervention further tests the proposed condition:
lookup fidelity improves when the attention interaction exposes a compatible
discrete $\operatorname{gate}(Q)\odot K$ contract, whereas unmodified
Spikformer remains a measured mismatch boundary. This is
contract-conditioned transfer, not unrestricted cross-architecture
generalization.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item We formulate spiking-attention lookup-addressability as a
  negative-controlled question covering occupancy, held-out conditional
  predictiveness, support reliability, and downstream dynamic fidelity.
  \item We introduce global-residual lookup with explicit hierarchical backoff
  and a subspace-decoupled Q/K estimator that preserves most full-address gain
  below a predeclared 25\% supported-entry budget.
  \item We introduce TCSLU, which restores time- and channel-conditioned current
  moments before BN/LIF. Under matched semantic controls it preserves QKFormer
  classification across CIFAR-10/100 and $T=1/4$ without treating small
  positive fluctuations as accuracy gains.
  \item We delimit the principle through calibration, checkpoint, corruption,
  replacement-scope, and QK-contract architecture interventions. The resulting
  claim is bounded to QK-compatible spiking-attention contracts and is kept
  separate from analytical cost proxies and operator-completeness audits.
\end{itemize}
