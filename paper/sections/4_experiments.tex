\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

and CIFAR-100. CIFAR-10 includes one $T=4$ checkpoint and three independently

scratch. All E3 experiments freeze the backbone and train only adapter

\subsection{Split-Aware Reconstruction}
\label{sec:e1_results}

Table~\ref{tab:e1_recon} shows held-out response reconstruction. Address LUT
prototypes reduce MSE relative to the global mean on both datasets, by
$3.71\%$ on CIFAR-10 and $4.42\%$ on CIFAR-100. Each value is the mean over
three randomized 128-batch calibration subsets evaluated on the complete
validation split; the standard deviations are only $0.0038$ and $0.0042$
percentage points, respectively.
The mean reduction is largest in stage1 ($10.04\%$), remains positive in
stage2 ($3.96\%$), and is $1.85\%$ in stage3. The address LUT also consistently
beats the coarse token/channel LUT ($3.42\%$ overall), while shuffled address
alignment worsens MSE by $15.82\%$. Candidate/background means remain nearly
identical to the global mean.

For CIFAR-10, the address, token/channel, candidate/background, and shuffled
relative reductions are $3.7080\%$, $2.7941\%$, $0.0329\%$, and $-17.8260\%$.
The corresponding CIFAR-100 values are $4.4247\%$, $3.4246\%$, $0.0356\%$,
and $-15.8216\%$. The ordering holds for every calibration seed and in all
three stages on both datasets, supporting an address-specific reconstruction
claim rather than a generic table-capacity effect.

\subsection{Forward-Only Replacement Diagnostics}
\label{sec:e2_results}

Forward-only replacement is useful diagnostically but not sufficient as a final
method. Stage1-only replacement is slightly positive on full validation, while
stage2 and combined stage1+stage2 replacement are negative. Further control
sweeps show that global smoothing nearly matches address LUT in T=4 settings,
so the forward-only path alone does not establish an address-specific claim.

(Table~\ref{tab:e3_centered}). It still trails global mean in both accuracy and
loss, so the result isolates address alignment rather than establishing a new
best classifier.

\subsection{Current Claim Boundary}
\label{sec:claim_boundary}

These experiments support the claim that Q/K binary addresses contain
measurable response structure and can support low-disturbance residual LUT
adapters. They do not support a checkpoint-stable address-specific accuracy
gain: global smoothing is a strong baseline, wins every CIFAR-100 residual-scale
setting, and the aligned-versus-shuffled ordering is not scale-stable. They
also do not support claims about hardware speed, energy reduction, ImageNet
accuracy, or a production full-LUT QKFormer wrapper.
