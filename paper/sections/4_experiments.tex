\subsection{Split-Aware Reconstruction}
\label{sec:e1_results}

Table~\ref{tab:e1_recon} shows held-out response reconstruction. Address LUT
prototypes reduce MSE relative to the global mean on both datasets, by
$3.71\%$ on CIFAR-10 and $4.42\%$ on CIFAR-100. Each value is the mean over
three randomized 128-batch calibration subsets evaluated on the complete
validation split; the standard deviations are only $0.0038$ and $0.0042$
percentage points, respectively.
The mean reduction is largest in stage1 ($10.04\%$), remains positive in
stage2 ($3.96\%$), and is $1.85\%$ in stage3. The address LUT also consistently
beats the coarse token/channel LUT ($3.42\%$ overall), while shuffled address
alignment worsens MSE by $15.82\%$. Candidate/background means remain nearly
identical to the global mean.
The independently trained CIFAR-100 $T=4$ checkpoint preserves the same
ordering: address lookup reduces MSE by $2.84\%$, compared with $2.24\%$ for
token/channel lookup, while shuffled alignment degrades it by $13.74\%$.

relative reductions are $3.7080\%$, $2.7941\%$, $0.0329\%$, and $-17.8260\%$.

claim rather than a generic table-capacity effect.

\subsection{Hierarchical Backoff Under Calibration Scarcity}
\label{sec:e5_results}

Table~\ref{tab:e5_backoff} evaluates the support-aware hierarchical backoff
LUT on CIFAR-100 $T=1$ with calibration sizes from 1 to 512 batches and
calibration seeds 42--44. The hierarchy is explicit about fallback behavior:
the fallback fractions sum to one for every run, and no validation sample is
silently assigned to an unsupported missing address. From 8 batches onward,
hierarchical backoff beats token/channel lookup for every seed and retains at
least $80\%$ of the full-address reconstruction gain. Shuffled full-address
lookup remains strongly worse than the correct hierarchy at every size,
confirming that address--prototype alignment matters.

The hierarchical gate outcome is therefore \emph{partial}. The method passes
the reconstruction and interpretability checks: 21/21 runs completed, the
hierarchy beats token/channel for all calibration sizes $\geq 8$, shuffled
lookup is always worse, fallback distributions are valid, and the hierarchy
retains the full-address gain at practical calibration sizes. It fails the
compact-table gate because effective supported entries are $40.4\%$--$51.1\%$
of the compact full-address space for sizes $\geq 8$.

Table~\ref{tab:e7_subspace} then evaluates the subspace-decoupled residual LUT.
The compact token/channel+Q/gate estimator beats token/channel lookup for every
calibration size and seed while using only $8.83\%$ supported entries. Adding
the K subtable remains below the $25\%$ entry budget and retains most of the
full-address gain. Both subspace variants beat their shuffled-subspace controls
in every run, showing that the gain depends on aligned Q/K sub-address
semantics rather than merely on residual table values.

\subsection{Downstream Accuracy Gate}
\label{sec:e3_results}

Reconstruction alone does not establish a useful classifier. We therefore
evaluate a registered CIFAR-100 $T=4$ gate on the best 81.23\% QKFormer
checkpoint. The gate fixes stage1, $\alpha=0.025$, two adapter epochs, 128
calibration and training batches, three adapter seeds, and the upstream AMP
full-validation loader. The frozen backbone is kept in evaluation mode, and an
$\alpha=0$ identity preflight must reproduce the checkpoint before any adapter
comparison is admitted. We compare address, global, token/channel, and
same-capacity shuffled-address probes. Detailed forward-only E2 diagnostics are
reported in the supplementary material because they motivate this gate but do
not support the central reconstruction claim.

Table~\ref{tab:t4_accuracy_gate} reports the centered residual decomposition,
which is the strongest registered address-aware variant. The paired backbone
reevaluation is $81.21\%$ Acc@1 (the training log reports a best checkpoint
value of $81.23\%$). The aligned LUT reaches $81.35\%$, $81.56\%$, and
$80.75\%$ across adapter seeds, for a mean of $81.22\%$. Thus its best run
exceeds the saved QKFormer result, but the effect is not seed-stable. The
same-capacity shuffled control averages $81.02\%$, while global smoothing
averages $81.15\%$.

The oracle analysis explains why further post-hoc adapter tuning is not
warranted. Although an accuracy oracle can recover $3.37$ points with the
aligned residual, shuffled and global controls recover $3.33$ and $3.21$
points. Hence most oracle headroom is generic sample selection rather than
Q/K-address-specific routing. We classify the downstream result as
\emph{conditional}: it shows a reproducible aligned--shuffled mean separation
and a strong single run, but not a stable accuracy improvement or a justified
learned gate.

\subsection{Current Claim Boundary}
\label{sec:claim_boundary}

These experiments support the claim that Q/K binary addresses contain
measurable response structure and that semantic subspace tables can retain most
of this structure under a fixed entry-count budget. Reconstruction does not by
itself establish downstream utility, and only protocol-v4 runs that preserve
the frozen backbone state and reproduce the upstream evaluation protocol are
admitted to the accuracy gate. The evidence does not support claims about
stable classification improvement, hardware speed, energy reduction, ImageNet
accuracy, or a production full-LUT QKFormer wrapper.
