\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

We evaluate on CIFAR-10 using the QKFormer CIFAR architecture from the upstream
implementation~\cite{zhou2024qkformer}. Unless otherwise stated, diagnostics
use the trained checkpoint with $T=4$ that reaches $96.08\%$ best validation
top-1 accuracy, and the $T=1$ checkpoint that reaches $95.20\%$ best validation
top-1 accuracy. All E3 adapter experiments freeze the trained QKFormer
backbone and train only the adapter table parameters.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\footnotesize

\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Checkpoint & Time steps & Best Acc@1 & Final Acc@1 \\
\midrule
QKFormer CIFAR-10 & 4 & 96.08 & 95.73 \\
QKFormer CIFAR-10 & 1 & 95.20 & 94.64 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
}
\caption{QKFormer baselines used for QK-LUTFormer diagnostics. Accuracy is
reported in percent on CIFAR-10 validation.}
\label{tab:qkformer_baselines}
\end{table}

\subsection{Address Feasibility}
\label{sec:address_feasibility}

Table~\ref{tab:e0_feasibility} reports Q/K address occupancy diagnostics. The
$T=1$ checkpoint keeps similar address coverage to $T=4$ while lowering
conditional response variance, making it a useful stress setting for testing
whether the address boundary itself carries structure.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Setting & Coverage & Singleton frac. & Cond. var. \\
\midrule
$T=4$ & 0.5993 & 0.0865 & 0.0713 \\
$T=1$ & 0.5805 & 0.0794 & 0.0549 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{E0 address feasibility diagnostics. Lower conditional variance
indicates that responses are more predictable within Q/K-derived buckets.}
\label{tab:e0_feasibility}
\end{table}

\subsection{Forward-Only Replacement Diagnostics}
\label{sec:e2_results}

Forward-only replacement is useful diagnostically but not sufficient as a final
method. Stage1-only replacement is slightly positive on full validation, while
stage2 and combined stage1+stage2 replacement are negative. Further control
sweeps show that global smoothing nearly matches address LUT in T=4 settings,
so the forward-only path alone does not establish an address-specific claim.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
Targets & Baseline Acc@1 & Replacement Acc@1 \\
\midrule
Stage1 only & 95.73 & \textbf{95.88} \\
Stage2 only & 95.73 & 95.59 \\
Stage1+Stage2 & 95.73 & 95.54 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{E2 full-validation forward-only replacement. Accuracy is in percent.
The result motivates using trainable residual adapters rather than pure
prototype replacement.}
\label{tab:e2_stage}
\end{table}

\subsection{Trainable Residual LUT Adapter}
\label{sec:e3_results}

The conservative E3 adapter uses fixed $\alpha=0.1$, two training epochs, 128
calibration batches, 128 training batches, full validation, and target module
\texttt{stage1.0.tssa}. Table~\ref{tab:e3_t1_multiseed} reports the $T=1$
multi-seed control sweep. The Q/K-address LUT is the only mode with positive
mean top-1 delta and it also has the best mean loss delta.

\subsection{Current Claim Boundary}
\label{sec:claim_boundary}

These experiments support the claim that Q/K binary addresses contain useful
structure for small residual LUT adapters in a $T=1$ boundary setting. They do
not yet support claims about hardware speed, energy reduction, ImageNet
accuracy, or a production full-LUT QKFormer wrapper. An independent
checkpoint-seed replication is running to test whether the T=1 adapter signal
survives a different trained backbone.
