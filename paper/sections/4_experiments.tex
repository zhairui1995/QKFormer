\subsection{Split-Aware Reconstruction}
\label{sec:e1_results}

Table~\ref{tab:e1_recon} shows held-out response reconstruction. Address LUT
prototypes reduce MSE relative to the global mean on both datasets, by
$3.71\%$ on CIFAR-10 and $4.42\%$ on CIFAR-100. Each value is the mean over
three randomized 128-batch calibration subsets evaluated on the complete
validation split; the standard deviations are only $0.0038$ and $0.0042$
percentage points, respectively.
The mean reduction is largest in stage1 ($10.04\%$), remains positive in
stage2 ($3.96\%$), and is $1.85\%$ in stage3. The address LUT also consistently
beats the coarse token/channel LUT ($3.42\%$ overall), while shuffled address
alignment worsens MSE by $15.82\%$. Candidate/background means remain nearly
identical to the global mean.
The independently trained CIFAR-100 $T=4$ checkpoint preserves the same
ordering: address lookup reduces MSE by $2.84\%$, compared with $2.24\%$ for
token/channel lookup, while shuffled alignment degrades it by $13.74\%$.

\subsection{Hierarchical Backoff Under Calibration Scarcity}
\label{sec:e5_results}

Table~\ref{tab:e5_backoff} evaluates the support-aware hierarchical backoff
LUT on CIFAR-100 $T=1$ with calibration sizes from 1 to 512 batches and
calibration seeds 42--44. The hierarchy is explicit about fallback behavior:
the fallback fractions sum to one for every run, and no validation sample is
silently assigned to an unsupported missing address. From 8 batches onward,
hierarchical backoff beats token/channel lookup for every seed and retains at
least $80\%$ of the full-address reconstruction gain. Shuffled full-address
lookup remains strongly worse than the correct hierarchy at every size,
confirming that address--prototype alignment matters.

The hierarchical gate outcome is therefore \emph{partial}. The method passes
the reconstruction and interpretability checks: 21/21 runs completed, the
hierarchy beats token/channel for all calibration sizes $\geq 8$, shuffled
lookup is always worse, fallback distributions are valid, and the hierarchy
retains the full-address gain at practical calibration sizes. It fails the
compact-table gate because effective supported entries are $40.4\%$--$51.1\%$
of the compact full-address space for sizes $\geq 8$.

subspace-decoupled residual LUT.

The detailed component analysis is fixed to seed 42 rather than introducing a
new random-seed sweep. Existing E7 results over calibration seeds 42--44 show

favorable run.

\subsection{End-to-End Current-Replacement Fidelity}
\label{sec:e3_results}

Table~\ref{tab:current_replacement_main} reports hard current replacement on
the complete validation split. Every row uses the seed-42 checkpoint, 128
calibration batches, the \texttt{stage1.0.tssa} target, and a 2,048-entry
aligned Q/K table. Static lookup is already close on CIFAR-10 $T=1$ and $T=4$
but drops by $13.07$ points on CIFAR-100 $T=4$. Moment/channel-calibrated lookup
reduces the worst observed loss to $0.52$ points and keeps all four settings
within $0.52$ points of the clean hook. The $+0.11$ CIFAR-10 $T=4$ difference
is treated as no-loss fluctuation, not as an accuracy improvement.

We repeated the aligned, global-mean, and token/channel controls with five
fixed 128-batch calibration subsets while holding the checkpoint, target,
validation set, and hyperparameters constant. Mean Acc@1 is $95.820\%$,
$95.630\%$, and $95.542\%$, respectively. The paired aligned-minus-global
difference is $+0.190$ points with a 95\% interval of
$[+0.037,+0.343]$, and aligned-minus-token/channel is $+0.278$ points
with interval $[+0.014,+0.542]$. Both intervals exclude zero, and aligned
lookup ranks first in all five subsets. This is statistically consistent
evidence for aligned lookup under calibration-subset variation. In contrast,
global-minus-token/channel is only $+0.088$ points with interval
$[-0.045,+0.221]$, so we do not interpret that single-subset ordering as
meaningful. The inference remains scoped to one checkpoint and one fixed
validation set.

The intervention also extends beyond a single target. Replacing stage1 and
stage2 currents together gives $95.43\%$ Acc@1 versus $95.70\%$ clean, whereas
the uncalibrated static table falls to $88.99\%$. Under mild corruption, the
calibrated replacement differs from the corresponding clean-hook model by
$0.01$ points for noise and $0.06$ points for brightness.
Minimum support thresholds 1, 2, and 4 produce the same $95.81\%$ result because
all 2,048 entries satisfy the thresholds under this calibration budget.

The compact response-prototype LUT does not directly scale to all four
attention projections. Simultaneously replacing
\texttt{stage1.0.tssa}, \texttt{stage2.0.tssa},
\texttt{stage3.0.ssa}, and \texttt{stage3.1.ssa} with calibrated prototypes
reduces CIFAR-100 Acc@1 to $56.28\%$ at $T=1$ and $54.08\%$ at $T=4$.
The grouped binary-pattern projection LUT removes this SSA approximation error
(Table~\ref{tab:all_attention_projection_lut}). The 2-bit configuration keeps
the aggregate current MSE near $2\times10^{-9}$ and preserves accuracy within
$0.06$ points on both checkpoints.

For one time step, the four original projections contain 2,359,296 MACs per
sample. The 2-bit implementation performs 6,144 vector lookups, where each
lookup returns all output-channel contributions for one input group. The
lookup-count/MAC-count ratio is $0.002604$, while scalar table-value reads equal
one half of the original MAC count. This is an analytical proxy that excludes
address generation, accumulation, memory hierarchy, BN/LIF, and measured
hardware cycles or energy.

\subsection{Cross-Architecture Generalization}
\label{sec:cross_arch_generalization}

Table~\ref{tab:cross_arch_generalization} keeps the QKFormer fidelity rows
separate from the Spikformer transfer result. On Spikformer-4-384w
\cite{zhou2023spikformer}, static SSA-current lookup reduces Acc@1 from
$88.16\%$ to $66.47\%$. Learned temporal+channel calibration recovers accuracy
to $76.35\%$, an $11.81$-point drop from clean. This is meaningful recovery
over static lookup, but it does not pass a near-lossless gate. We therefore
report partial transfer and an architecture-dependent boundary, not broad
Spiking Transformer generalization.

Table~\ref{tab:cross_arch_controls} exposes the missing matched controls rather
than treating them as completed. Table~\ref{tab:replacement_robustness}
separates completed QKFormer robustness rows from pending Spikformer cells.
Table~\ref{tab:lookup_cost_proxy} reports metadata-aware analytical storage and
operation-count proxies, and Table~\ref{tab:peer_comparison} places the
replacement unit beside reported Spiking Transformer backbones without
presenting unlike datasets as a direct accuracy ranking.

\subsection{Current Claim Boundary}
\label{sec:claim_boundary}

These experiments support the claim that Q/K binary addresses contain
measurable response structure and that semantic subspace tables retain most of
this structure under a fixed entry-count budget. They additionally establish
near-lossless QKFormer current replacement under the reported single- and
two-target protocols, and near-exact replacement of all four attention
projection currents with grouped spike-pattern LUTs. Spikformer shows partial
transfer but remains an architecture-dependent boundary, so cross-architecture
generalization is not established. The evidence does not support hardware speed,
measured SRAM, energy reduction, ImageNet accuracy, replacement of Q/K/V or
MLP operators, replacement of every QKFormer computation, or a production
full-LUT wrapper.
