\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We studied whether QKFormer's binary Q/K spike vectors form a useful LUT
address space. Q/K-derived addresses are well occupied on CIFAR-10 and
CIFAR-100 and improve held-out reconstruction over global averages. Address
alignment also separates from same-capacity shuffled controls in several
low-disturbance adapter settings. The negative evidence is equally important:
the classification effect changes across checkpoints and stages, and global
smoothing often matches or exceeds the address LUT. A centered decomposition
separates aligned from shuffled address residuals but still does not surpass
global smoothing. The supported result is
therefore a structured-address and constrained-fidelity story, not a
checkpoint-stable accuracy improvement or full hardware replacement story.
Future work should test whether the centered address residual remains useful
across residual scales and independent checkpoints. Real LUT-oriented latency
and energy should be measured only after that algorithmic gate is satisfied.
