\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We studied whether QKFormer's binary Q/K spike vectors form a useful LUT
address space. Q/K-derived addresses are well occupied on CIFAR-10 and
CIFAR-100 and improve held-out reconstruction over global averages. On
CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, the fine-grained address LUT consistently exceeds a
coarse token/channel LUT and strongly separates from a same-capacity shuffled
table across randomized calibration subsets. The address-over-token/channel
ordering also holds across three independently trained $T=1$ checkpoints and
from 8 to 512 calibration batches. The hierarchical backoff audit further
shows how this addressability can be made explicit under support thresholds:
fallback fractions are measurable, shuffled alignment remains negative, and the
hierarchy retains full-address reconstruction gains at practical calibration
sizes. However, the same result also prevents overclaiming, because active
compact-table entries remain above our compression gate.

The negative evidence is equally important: the classification effect changes
across checkpoints and stages, and global smoothing often matches or exceeds
the address LUT. A centered decomposition separates aligned from shuffled
address residuals but still does not surpass global smoothing. A residual-scale
sweep further shows that the aligned-versus-shuffled classification ordering is
not stable. The supported result is therefore an address-specific,
interpretable reconstruction story, not a checkpoint-stable accuracy
improvement or full hardware replacement story. Future work should test
budgeted table pruning, larger datasets, and real hardware or simulator
measurements before claiming LUT-oriented latency or energy benefits.
