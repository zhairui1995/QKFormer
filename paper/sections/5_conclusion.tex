\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We studied whether compact local states derived from QKFormer's binary Q/K
spikes form a useful LUT address space. Q/K-derived addresses are well occupied on CIFAR-10 and
CIFAR-100 and improve held-out reconstruction over global averages. On
CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, the fine-grained address LUT consistently exceeds a
coarse token/channel LUT and strongly separates from a same-capacity shuffled
table across randomized calibration subsets. The address-over-token/channel
ordering also holds across three independently trained $T=1$ checkpoints and
from 8 to 512 calibration batches. The hierarchical backoff audit further
shows how this addressability can be made explicit under support thresholds:
fallback fractions are measurable, shuffled alignment remains negative, and the
hierarchy retains full-address reconstruction gains at practical calibration
sizes. The subspace-decoupled residual LUT then addresses the compactness
failure of pure backoff: token/channel+Q/gate subtables improve over
token/channel lookup with only $8.83\%$ supported entries, and adding K remains
below the $25\%$ entry-budget gate while preserving most full-address
reconstruction gain.

The negative evidence is equally important: on CIFAR-100 $T=4$, an aligned
centered residual reaches a best single-run Acc@1 of $81.56\%$ but averages
$81.22\%$ across three adapter seeds, essentially matching the paired
$81.21\%$ backbone. Its accuracy-oracle gain exceeds the shuffled control by
only $0.04$ points. The local reconstruction ordering therefore does not
automatically induce a stable classification ordering. The supported result is
therefore an address-specific, interpretable and compact reconstruction story,
not a checkpoint-stable accuracy improvement or full hardware replacement
story. Future work should test larger datasets and real hardware or simulator
measurements before claiming LUT-oriented latency or energy benefits.
