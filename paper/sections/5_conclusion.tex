\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We studied whether QKFormer's binary Q/K spike vectors form a useful LUT
address space. The evidence is conditional but encouraging: Q/K-derived
addresses are well occupied, improve held-out reconstruction over global
averages, and expose a low-disturbance residual-adapter window in a T=1 stress
setting. The negative results are equally important: prototype replacement
alone is not enough, several T=4 controls nearly match address LUTs, and
larger residual weights can create seed-dependent drift. The current best
interpretation is therefore a carefully constrained trainable residual adapter
story, not a full hardware replacement story. Future work should validate the
result with shuffled-address controls, additional independent checkpoints,
larger datasets, additional stages, and real LUT-oriented efficiency
measurements.
