\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We studied whether QKFormer's binary Q/K spike vectors form a useful LUT
address space. The evidence is conditional but encouraging: Q/K-derived
addresses are well occupied, improve held-out reconstruction over global
averages, and produce a positive mean adapter gain against non-address controls
in a T=1 stress setting. The negative forward-only results are equally
important: prototype replacement alone is not enough, and several T=4 controls
nearly match address LUTs. The current best interpretation is therefore a
trainable residual adapter story, not a full hardware replacement story. Future
work should validate the result across independent trained checkpoints, larger
datasets, additional stages, and real LUT-oriented efficiency measurements.
