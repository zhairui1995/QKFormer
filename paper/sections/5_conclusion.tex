\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We studied whether QKFormer's binary Q/K spike vectors form a useful LUT
address space. Q/K-derived addresses are well occupied on CIFAR-10 and
CIFAR-100 and improve held-out reconstruction over global averages. On
CIFAR-100, the fine-grained address LUT consistently exceeds a coarse
token/channel LUT and strongly separates from a same-capacity shuffled table
across randomized calibration subsets. The negative evidence is equally important:
the classification effect changes across checkpoints and stages, and global
smoothing often matches or exceeds the address LUT. A centered decomposition
separates aligned from shuffled address residuals but still does not surpass
global smoothing. A residual-scale sweep further shows that the
aligned-versus-shuffled classification ordering is not stable. The supported
result is therefore an address-specific reconstruction story, not a
checkpoint-stable accuracy improvement or full hardware replacement story.
Future work should establish reconstruction stability across calibration
subsets and larger datasets before measuring real LUT-oriented latency and
energy.
