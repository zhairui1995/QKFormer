\section{QK-LUTFormer Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Lookup-Addressability Formulation}
\label{sec:addressability_formulation}

Let $s=(Q,K,G,h,t,c)$ denote the discrete internal state available at a
spiking-attention response, including query and key spikes, a Q-derived gate,
head, time, token, and channel context. QK-LUTFormer studies whether a
deterministic map
\begin{equation}
  a=\phi(s)
\end{equation}
turns that state into a useful response address. We call an address family
\emph{lookup-addressable} when it satisfies four progressively stronger
conditions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Occupancy}: addresses are repeatedly observed rather than
  dominated by singletons.
  \item \textbf{Conditional predictiveness}: address-conditioned prototypes
  improve held-out reconstruction beyond global and coarse controls.
  \item \textbf{Support reliability}: unseen or weakly supported addresses are
  exposed and handled by an explicit fallback rule.
  \item \textbf{Dynamic fidelity}: the retrieved current preserves downstream
  behavior after the stateful BN/LIF path.
\end{enumerate}
The first three conditions concern the address representation and estimator.
The fourth is deliberately separate: local reconstruction does not imply that
small distribution shifts will survive thresholded spiking dynamics.

\subsection{Code-Faithful Semantic Q/K Addresses}
\label{sec:qk_address}

We construct addresses from discrete states already present in QKFormer rather
than introducing learned anchor neurons. In particular, the current method
does not encode firing order among selected anchor neurons. It also does not
use an entire $d$-dimensional Q/K vector as one $2^{2d}$ truth-table address.
Each scalar response is indexed by a compact local state.

For Token-QK attention, the semantic tuple is
\begin{equation}
 a^{\mathrm{TQK}}=(h,b_t(i),b_c(j),g_q(h,i),k(h,j,i)),
\end{equation}
where $h$ is the head, $b_t$ and $b_c$ are token and within-head channel bins,
$g_q$ is the binary Q-derived token gate, and $k$ is the corresponding K
spike. For spike-form self-attention, we use
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 a^{\mathrm{SSA}}=(&h,b_t(i),b_c(j),q_{hij},k_{hij},\\
 &b_p(\lVert\mathbf q_{hi}\rVert_0),
 b_p(\lVert\mathbf k_{hi}\rVert_0)),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
which adds local Q/K bits and quantized population counts. With eight token
bins, eight channel bins, four population bins, and eight heads, the two
spaces contain 2,048 and 32,768 possible addresses.

The fields are packed with a deterministic mixed-radix map. Distinct field
tuples therefore do not incur hash collisions. The relevant failure modes are
instead \emph{quantization aliasing}, where different continuous contexts
share a discrete tuple, and \emph{support sparsity}, where a valid address is
unseen or weakly represented during calibration. Throughout the paper,
``full address'' refers to all fields of this compact construction, not a raw
full-vector bit pattern.

\subsection{Global-Residual Lookup and Support-Aware Backoff}
\label{sec:global_residual_backoff}

For a calibration response $y$, let $\mu=\mathbb{E}[y]$ be the global response
mean, $n_a$ the support count of address $a$, and
\begin{equation}
  R_a=\mathbb{E}[y\mid a]-\mu
\end{equation}
its address-conditioned residual. With minimum support $m$, the basic
global-residual estimator is
\begin{equation}
  \hat y(a)=\mu+\mathbb{I}[n_a\geq m]R_a.
  \label{eq:global_residual}
\end{equation}
An unsupported address therefore returns the global estimate instead of an
unvalidated prototype.

We extend this rule into a semantic hierarchy:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \mathrm{full}
 \rightarrow \mathrm{TC+QK}
 \rightarrow \mathrm{TC+Q}
 \rightarrow \mathrm{TC}
 \rightarrow \mathrm{global},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:backoff_chain}
\end{equation}
where TC denotes token/channel context. Lookup starts at the most specific
available level and backs off until its support threshold is met. The selected
level is recorded for every evaluation response, so an unsupported fine
address cannot silently masquerade as a successful full-address lookup.

This hierarchy is an uncertainty-handling mechanism, not by itself a
compression result. It can store several increasingly specific tables, so we
report fallback fractions, supported entries, and effective size alongside
held-out reconstruction.

\subsection{Subspace-Decoupled Residual LUT}
\label{sec:subspace_lut}

To avoid materializing a Cartesian full-address table, we decompose the
residual by semantic subspaces. Let
$\pi_{\mathrm{tc}}(a)$, $\pi_{\mathrm{q}}(a)$, and
$\pi_{\mathrm{k}}(a)$ project the address onto token/channel, Q-or-gate, and K
fields. For a selected set of subtables $\mathcal S$, the estimator is
\begin{equation}
  \hat y(a)=\mu+
  \frac{1}{|\mathcal S|}
  \sum_{s\in\mathcal S}
  \mathbb{I}[n_s(\pi_s(a))\geq m]
  R_s[\pi_s(a)].
  \label{eq:subspace_residual}
\end{equation}
We evaluate TC+Q/gate and TC+QK compositions. Unsupported residuals contribute
zero, preserving the global fallback in Eq.~\ref{eq:global_residual}.

If $\mathcal G_s$ is the domain of subtable $s$, a monolithic Cartesian table
scales as $\prod_s|\mathcal G_s|$, whereas the decomposition stores
$\sum_s|\mathcal G_s|$ entries. For $M$ balanced binary subspaces over $D$
bits, the nominal growth changes from $2^D$ to $M2^{D/M}$. A matched
shuffled-subspace control retains the same residual values and entry count but
permutes their address assignment.

Our compactness gate is defined over supported scalar entries. Prototype-only
and metadata-inclusive byte counts are reported as analytical storage proxies;
they are not measurements of GPU memory, packed SRAM, latency, or energy.

\subsection{TCSLU Dynamic Calibration}
\label{sec:tcslu}

Static semantic lookup returns a prototype current $\tilde y$, but its
time/channel statistics can differ from the original current even when local
MSE is small. TCSLU, the Temporal-Channel Calibrated Semantic Lookup Unit,
calibrates these statistics before the original BN/LIF path. For time step $t$
and channel $c$,
\begin{equation}
  \hat y_{t,c}
  =\gamma_{t,c}\tilde y_{t,c}+\beta_{t,c},
  \label{eq:tcslu}
\end{equation}
with calibration initialization
\begin{equation}
  \gamma_{t,c}=
  \frac{\sigma^{\mathrm{orig}}_{t,c}}
       {\sigma^{\mathrm{lut}}_{t,c}+\epsilon},
  \qquad
  \beta_{t,c}=
  \mu^{\mathrm{orig}}_{t,c}
  -\gamma_{t,c}\mu^{\mathrm{lut}}_{t,c}.
\end{equation}
The moments are estimated on training batches and classification is evaluated
on the complete validation split without optimizing the QKFormer backbone.
The implementation supports moment, temporal-only, channel-only, and combined
calibration modes; the combined TCSLU path retains support-aware fallback.

The complete conceptual flow is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{semantic address}
&\rightarrow \text{global residual/backoff}\\
&\rightarrow \text{subspace lookup}\\
&\rightarrow \text{temporal-channel calibration}\\
&\rightarrow \text{BN/LIF}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Thus address semantics determine what response pattern is retrieved, while
dynamic calibration determines whether that pattern is compatible with the
stateful spiking path.

\subsection{Evaluation Scope and Analytical Complexity}
\label{sec:method_scope}

The primary end-to-end intervention replaces selected QKFormer attention
projection currents while leaving the rest of the network unchanged. We compare
aligned lookup with shuffled, token/channel, global, and same-size random
controls under fixed calibration and validation protocols. A separate
QK-contract Spikformer intervention tests whether lookup fidelity depends on
the attention interaction rather than assuming identical address encodings
across architectures.

Entry counts, prototype bytes, metadata-aware bytes, and lookup/MAC ratios are
analytical proxies. They exclude memory hierarchy, address-generation cost,
control flow, measured cycles, and energy. Grouped projection and native
affine/BN/LIF replacement audits test operator-class fidelity and are summarized
as a completeness boundary in the main paper; their construction and detailed
rows are moved to Appendix~\ref{sec:supp_replacement}.
